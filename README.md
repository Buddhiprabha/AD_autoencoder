## Deep Learning of Plasma Proteomics Reveals a Continuous Molecular Axis in Alzheimer’s Disease

This repository contains the code related to the paper under review titled "Deep Learning of Plasma Proteomics Reveals a Continuous Molecular Axis in Alzheimer’s Disease" by Buddhiprabha Erabadda, Muhammad Ali, Hanjun Zhao, Carlos Cruchaga, Alejo Nevado‐Holgado, and Laura Winchester.

**INTRODUCTION**: We analysed plasma proteomic profiles from two cohorts derived from the Global Neurodegeneration Proteomics Consortium with the goal of identifying molecular subtypes in AD.

**METHODS**: We used a graph‐smoothed autoencoder to obtain a 128‐D latent representation of the SomaScan 7K proteomic space followed by K‐means clustering to characterise molecular heterogeneity (discovery: N=898, validation: N=1093).

**RESULTS**: We observed a disease continuum as opposed to discrete subtypes and identified two regions on the continuum that form a bipolar axis with Endolysosomal‐Translation characteristics. This axis replicated in an independently trained model, with up to 86% of the two poles’ cluster-exclusive proteins recovered in the matched cluster against 10–27% in non‐matched clusters. We also observed a third cluster with Complement‐Coagulation Activation with Metabolic‐Proteostatic Suppression characteristics, which formed in all five discovery runs but only in two of five validation runs.

**DISCUSSION**: Molecular heterogeneity previously described in CSF and post‐mortem brain is detectable in plasma, and defines an axis along which patients could in principle be positioned without lumbar puncture or tissue access. Comparable structure was detectable in cognitively normal indi viduals, indicating that the axis describes a dimension of variation that is not confined to disease, as previously reported for CSF subtypes.
